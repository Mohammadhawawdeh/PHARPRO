How a project runs

A defensible path from design to routine use

  1. Define grade & use pointsURS states which grade each use point needs and why, including the most remote point on the loop.
  2. Design qualificationGeneration, storage, distribution and sanitisation confirmed capable of the required grade.
  3. IQInstalled system verified against design: materials, welds, slope, dead legs, instruments and documentation.
  4. OQFull operating range challenged, including sanitisation cycles and alarm and interlock response.
  5. Three-phase PQIntensive sampling, consistency confirmation and a seasonal year of monitoring.
  6. Routine monitoringTrended, risk-based monitoring and clear requalification triggers in your quality system.
Performance qualification

The three-phase sampling plan

Water systems are qualified over time, not in a single sampling round. Typical durations are below; the final plan is justified for your system in the protocol.

PhaseTypical durationPurpose
Phase 12-4 weeks, daily samplingCharacterise the system at every use point and critical loop location, and refine operating parameters.
Phase 22-4 weeks, daily samplingConfirm consistent performance once parameters and procedures are fixed.
Phase 3About 1 year, reduced frequencyCapture seasonal variation in feed water, ambient temperature and usage before routine monitoring frequency is set.

Frequently asked questions

Water system validation FAQs

How long does the three-phase water system validation take?

Phase 1 typically runs two to four weeks of intensive daily sampling, Phase 2 a further two to four weeks confirming consistency, and Phase 3 extends over approximately one year to capture seasonal variation before routine monitoring frequency is set.

Is Purified Water validation different from WFI validation?

The qualification approach of DQ, IQ, OQ and three-phase PQ is the same. The generation technology, storage and distribution design and the acceptance criteria differ, since WFI carries an endotoxin limit and stricter microbial expectations than Purified Water.

What triggers requalification of a water system?

Significant changes to generation, storage or distribution equipment, new or relocated use points, repeated excursions, major component replacement or an extended shutdown should each trigger a documented impact assessment and requalification proportionate to the change.

Can you review a water system that is already in use?

Yes. PHARPRO can perform a gap assessment of existing qualification records, monitoring data and trending, and define the remediation or requalification needed before an inspection.

Do you run the sampling and laboratory testing?

PHARPRO writes the sampling plan, trains and supports your team or your contract laboratory on execution, and reviews and reports the results. Laboratory analysis is performed by your QC laboratory or an accredited contract laboratory.
