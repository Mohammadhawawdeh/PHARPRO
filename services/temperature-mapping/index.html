How a study runs

From protocol to approved report

Mapping is only defensible when the study design is justified before the first logger is placed.

  1. Scope & risk reviewDrawings, HVAC layout, racking, doors, operating data and excursion history define the study boundary and risk locations.
  2. Protocol & acceptance criteriaLogger count and positions, study duration, loading conditions, challenge tests and acceptance limits, approved before execution.
  3. Calibrated logger placementCalibrated data loggers with traceable certificates, placed at corners, centre, supply and return air, doors and every racking level.
  4. Execution & challenge testsEmpty and loaded studies, door-opening, power-failure and recovery tests, alarm verification and seasonal studies where required.
  5. AnalysisHot and cold spots, stability, excursions, mean kinetic temperature where relevant and where monitoring probes must go.
  6. Report & requalification planInspection-ready report with conclusions, qualified storage volume, operating limitations and the requalification interval.

Frequently asked questions

Temperature mapping FAQs

When is temperature mapping required?

Before a temperature-controlled storage area is first used, after significant changes such as new HVAC, cooling equipment or racking layout, after recurring excursions, and periodically according to a justified requalification strategy.

How many data loggers are needed for a cold room or warehouse?

There is no single number. Logger quantity and placement are justified by volume, geometry, airflow, racking, doors and risk locations. Typical cold rooms of 10 to 50 m³ need around 12 to 15 loggers; warehouses need a three-dimensional grid across racking levels.

How long does a temperature mapping study take?

Cold rooms and freezers are usually mapped for 24 to 72 hours per loading condition. Ambient warehouses are usually mapped for 3 to 7 days per season, covering summer and winter extremes.

How much does temperature mapping cost?

Cold room temperature mapping studies start from USD 2,500. Warehouses are quoted on volume, logger count, number of seasons and travel. PHARPRO provides fixed-price proposals with no hourly billing.

Does mapping replace continuous temperature monitoring?

No. Mapping characterises temperature distribution and shows where routine monitoring probes belong. Continuous monitoring provides the ongoing evidence during routine operation.

Do you also qualify the equipment and HVAC?

Yes. Mapping is often combined with IQ/OQ/PQ of cold rooms, freezers and HVAC under our CQV services, and with GSDP procedures and training for warehouses.
