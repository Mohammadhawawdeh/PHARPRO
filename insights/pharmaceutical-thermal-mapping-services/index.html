Thermal Mapping · CQV

Pharmaceutical Thermal Mapping Services: Warehouses, Cold Rooms and Controlled Areas

Thermal mapping provides documented evidence that a temperature-controlled space maintains suitable conditions throughout its usable volume. PHARPRO now delivers risk-based thermal mapping studies for pharmaceutical warehouses, cold rooms, freezers, stability chambers, transport equipment and other controlled environments.

By Mohammad Awawdeh · Published and reviewed 1 September 2026

When is thermal mapping required?

Mapping is typically performed before routine use, after significant changes, and periodically according to a justified lifecycle strategy. Common triggers include new HVAC arrangements, changes to storage layout, replacement of cooling equipment, recurring temperature excursions or expansion of the qualified storage volume.

The study should reflect intended operating conditions and the risks created by doors, walls, ceilings, supply and return air, racking, product loading, defrost cycles, power interruption and seasonal weather.

What a PHARPRO mapping project includes

PHARPRO defines the study boundary, reviews available drawings and operating data, develops the protocol, identifies risk-based logger positions and establishes acceptance criteria before execution. Calibrated data loggers are deployed across representative high-risk and low-risk locations.

Execution can include empty and loaded studies, door-opening challenges, power-failure or recovery tests, alarm verification and seasonal studies where required. Results are reviewed for hot and cold spots, stability, excursions, recovery and sensor placement implications.

Deliverables you can use in an inspection

The final package includes the approved protocol, logger and calibration traceability, location map, raw and processed data, temperature profiles, deviation assessment, conclusions and recommendations. Where a permanent monitoring system is used, the mapping outcome supports routine sensor placement and alarm strategy.

The objective is not merely to produce charts. It is to give the quality unit a clear, defensible conclusion about the qualified storage volume, operational limitations and actions needed to maintain control.

Facilities and equipment covered

PHARPRO can scope mapping for ambient pharmaceutical warehouses, cold rooms, freezers, stability chambers, incubators, ovens and temperature-controlled transport configurations. The exact approach is adapted to intended use, product risk, operating range and applicable procedures.

Projects are available in Jordan and across MENA, with protocol development, data review and reporting support also available remotely where local execution resources are qualified.

Frequently asked questions

How many data loggers are needed?

There is no universal number. Quantity and placement should be justified by room size, geometry, airflow, storage configuration, risk locations and study objectives.

How long should a thermal mapping study run?

The duration should capture representative operating cycles and foreseeable challenges. Warehouses commonly require longer studies than small chambers or controlled equipment.

Does mapping replace continuous monitoring?

No. Mapping characterizes temperature distribution and supports routine sensor placement. Continuous monitoring provides ongoing evidence during routine operation.
